What the weekend actually is

The Walk to Emmaus is a three-day experience of Christian spiritual renewal, a ministry of The Upper Room. It takes its name from Luke 24, where two travelers walk a long road with a stranger and only recognize Jesus afterward, at the table, in the breaking of the bread.

You come as a pilgrim. You are not asked to teach, perform, or have your faith worked out in advance. You listen, you eat, you worship, and you sit with the same handful of people at the same table from Thursday to Sunday. Holy Communion is shared each day at a table open to all who love Christ.

The weekend ends Sunday evening, and then the part that lasts begins. Emmaus calls it the Fourth Day — the rest of your life, walked with a small reunion group and a community that keeps showing up.

The rhythm of the weekend

  1. Thursday eveningArrive, share supper, meet the people at your table. The evening ends in worship and a quiet night.
  2. FridayTalks, table conversation, worship, Communion. The day is full, and the pace is not yours to manage — that is part of the gift.

Three things to know before you fill this out

1

You need a sponsor

Every pilgrim comes sponsored by someone who has already made a Walk. Your sponsor gets you there, prays for you through the weekend, and quietly looks after whatever you left at home. No sponsor yet? Say so on the form and [COMMUNITY] will connect you with one.

2

The weekends are separate

Men and women attend on different dates. Couples often go on back-to-back weekends — note it on the form and the registrar will help you sort out which one comes first and who is home with the kids.

3

Cost should not decide this

[$000] covers lodging, every meal, and materials for the three days. Scholarships are available and handled confidentially, and no one is turned away over money. There is a box for this on the form; checking it is not a small thing to ask.

Questions people actually ask

Do I have to be United Methodist?

No. The Walk to Emmaus is a ministry of The Upper Room and grew out of the United Methodist tradition, but it is open to anyone who is part of a Christian congregation. Pilgrims come from churches all over [AREA].

Can I come late or leave early?

The weekend is built as one unbroken seventy-two hours, and pilgrims are asked to stay for all of it — that is most of what makes it work. If these dates cannot hold you for three days, register for the next one; [COMMUNITY] holds [N] weekends a year.

What do I bring?

Comfortable clothes for three days, toiletries, a Bible if you use one, and anything you take daily. A full packing list comes with your confirmation. [PACKING LIST LINK]

Do I keep my phone?

Pilgrims are asked to set phones and watches aside for the weekend, so the days are not measured out in advance. [RETREAT CENTER] has a number for emergencies, and it goes home with your family. [CONFIRM THIS IS YOUR COMMUNITY'S PRACTICE]

What happens afterward?

Your Fourth Day. Most pilgrims join a reunion group — three or four people who meet weekly for an hour — and come to community gatherings. None of it is required. The invitation just stays open.

Still deciding?

Call or write. Nobody will pressure you, and asking is not a commitment.
